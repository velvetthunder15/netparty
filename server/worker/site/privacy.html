NetParty

Privacy Policy

Last updated 12 November 2026 (live streams on YouTube, Twitch, Kick and Rumble in NetParty 2.5)

NetParty is a free browser extension that lets friends watch a video together in sync, with text, voice and video chat, GIFs and emoji reactions. It works on Netflix, JioHotstar, YouTube, Prime Video, HBO Max, Crunchyroll, SonyLIV, Hulu, Disney+, Paramount+, STARZ and Tubi, on live streams on YouTube, Twitch, Kick and Rumble, and on other video sites you choose. It is run by Chirag Rasane. This policy explains what the extension and its sync server handle, and why.

No accounts, no ads, no tracking code

NetParty has no sign-up and no accounts. The extension and this website have no analytics, advertising or tracking code and set no cookies, and we never sell or share your data. The only measuring we do is the anonymous daily totals described below, counted on our own sync server.

Anonymous usage totals

To know roughly how many people use NetParty, the sync server counts a few totals for each day (midnight to midnight, India time):

  • People that day. To count each person once without keeping anything about them, the server mixes your IP address and browser type (the user agent your browser sends) with a random value that is made fresh every day, and turns that into a code that can't be turned back (a salted hash). It only checks whether it has already seen that code today. When the day ends, the random value and all of that day's codes are deleted, so they can't be linked to you or to another day. Only the number of people is kept.
  • Parties. How many parties were started and how many times someone joined one.
  • Peak sizes. The most people connected at the same time, and the size of the biggest party.
  • Which service. How many parties started on each service (such as Netflix, YouTube or a video on another site), worked out from the kind of video ID the server already receives to keep the party in sync. Which title it was is not recorded.

Only these totals are kept, for up to 120 days, and then deleted. They are never linked to a person, a party code, a name, a chat message, a picture or a video. No cookies or identifiers are stored on your device for this, nothing new is sent by the extension, and no third party is involved: the counting runs on our server and only we can see the totals.

End-to-end encryption

Party chat is end-to-end encrypted. When a party starts, the host's browser creates a random 256-bit key. The key is part of the invite link, after the # (browsers never send that part of a link to any server), and is kept only in the browsers of the people in the party, for that tab, until they leave. Chat messages (including mentions, spoilers, edits and deletes), GIFs, reactions, the pinned message, typing indicators, "my video is loading" notices, display names and profile pictures, the setup messages for voice calls, and the page address of a video on another site, are encrypted with this key (AES-256-GCM) before they leave your browser. Our sync server only ever receives, relays and stores the encrypted form, and cannot read it. Anyone who has the full invite link can join the party and read its chat, so share the link only with people you want there.

The sync server can still see what it needs to run the party: the party code, a random member ID for each person, when messages are sent and roughly how large they are (sizes are rounded up), playback state (play, pause, position, and the ID of the video being watched, which the invite page also uses), how many people are in the party, whose connection has dropped (so the party can pause for them), whether the host has locked the party and whom the host removed (so they can't come back), and connection data such as IP addresses.

What the extension handles

  • Display name and picture. The name you choose and your profile picture, shown to others in your party: an emoji you pick (such as 🐼 or 🍕), one of the pictures that come with the extension, or a photo you upload. Both are stored in your browser and sent to the people in your party end-to-end encrypted; the sync server only passes on the encrypted form.
  • Profile photo (optional). If you upload a photo, your browser crops it and shrinks it to a small 96 x 96 picture on your device; the original file is never uploaded anywhere. The small picture is kept in this browser only (it is not synced) and is sent inside your end-to-end encrypted profile, so only the people in your party can see it. The sync server stores it only in that encrypted form, with the rest of the party's encrypted data, and deletes it with the party. Remove photo deletes it from your browser and stops sending it.
  • Chat messages. What you type or send in the party chat (text, emoji, links and GIFs), end-to-end encrypted and relayed by the sync server to everyone in that party. This includes replies (a reply carries a short quote of the message it answers: the sender's name and up to 80 characters, or "GIF") and reactions on chat messages (the emoji and which message it's on). Both are part of the encrypted chat and are stored, encrypted, in the party's chat history like any message. The same goes for @mentions (which party member a mention names), spoilers (the hidden text travels encrypted with the message and is blurred on screen until each person taps it) and edits and deletes of your own messages (an edit or delete is a new encrypted line in the history; people using NetParty older than 2.2 keep seeing the original message).
  • Pinned message. When a message is pinned, a short encrypted copy (who wrote it and up to 80 characters, or "Spoiler" or "GIF") is kept with the party so people who join later see it. The server stores only the encrypted form, and replaces or removes it when someone pins another message or unpins it.
  • Live stream delay. In a party on a live stream, your browser tells the people in your party every few seconds how far behind live your video is playing, so everyone can watch the same moment. This is end-to-end encrypted like the notices below, and not stored.
  • Typing and loading notices. While you type in the chat, and while your video is stuck loading in a party, short end-to-end encrypted notices are relayed to the people in your party so they see "is typing" or "Waiting for you to load". These notices are not stored.
  • Reactions over the video. When you tap a reaction in the 🎉 tray, that emoji is end-to-end encrypted and relayed to the people in your party so it floats over their video. These floating reactions are not stored.
  • GIF searches. When you open the GIF picker, your search terms (suggested from the title you're watching) are sent to our server, which asks KLIPY for matching GIFs. Searches can't be end-to-end encrypted, because KLIPY has to read them to find GIFs. KLIPY receives the search terms from our server, not your name or party, and our server doesn't log them. Results are cached on our server's edge for about 10 minutes, without recording who searched. The GIF images themselves load from KLIPY's servers, so KLIPY can see your IP address as any website would, under its own privacy policy. A GIF you send is shared in the (encrypted) party chat as a link to KLIPY's image.
  • Page address (videos on other sites). When you sync a video on a site other than the supported streaming services, the address of the page you're on and the video's length are shared with the people in your party, so they can open the same page and check it's the same video. The address is end-to-end encrypted with the party's key. Our server only relays and stores the encrypted form and can't read it; for these parties the server sees a random video ID instead of a title ID. The invite page decrypts the address in your friend's browser and only links to ordinary http or https pages.
  • Party and playback state. The party code, the ID of what's playing (a Netflix, JioHotstar, YouTube, Prime Video, HBO Max, Crunchyroll, SonyLIV, Hulu, Disney+, Paramount+, STARZ, Tubi or Rumble title ID, a Twitch or Kick channel name, a YouTube or Rumble live stream's ID, or a random ID for a video on another site), and play, pause, seek and playback speed events with timestamps, so everyone stays in sync. These are not encrypted: the server needs them to keep everyone in sync and to send invited friends to the right title.
  • Voice and video chat. Voice and video run peer to peer between party members over WebRTC, which encrypts the audio and video (DTLS-SRTP). When two networks can't connect directly, the call goes through a relay (TURN) server run by Cloudflare. The relay only forwards the encrypted audio and video and can't play it; like any network service it sees the IP addresses of the two ends. Our server hands out short-lived relay credentials and only passes on the connection setup messages that let browsers find each other, and those are end-to-end encrypted too, so the server can't read the network addresses inside them. Your audio and video are not sent to or recorded by our server. While you're in voice chat, NetParty can lower the volume of the video you're watching while a friend speaks (it only listens to whether someone is speaking, which voice chat already shows); this happens in your browser and nothing about it is sent anywhere. Microphone access is requested only when you turn voice on. The camera is used only when you turn it on, and stops when you turn it off or leave voice.
  • Host controls. The host can make someone else the host, remove someone from the party, and lock the party so no one new can join. To keep a removed person's tab from rejoining, the sync server keeps that tab's reconnect code (a random code the tab makes for itself, which other members never see; it says nothing about the person), and whether the party is locked, with the party, and deletes both with the party.
  • Party recap. The recap image (what you watched, for how long, who was there, and how many messages and reactions there were) is made in your browser from what it already shows you. Its running totals are kept in your browser's memory for that tab and deleted when the party ends or the tab closes. Nothing about it is sent to us or anyone else. If you download or copy the image, sharing it is up to you.
  • Sharing the invite link. The WhatsApp, Discord and Instagram buttons copy the invite link to your clipboard and open that app's own website for you to paste it; the link is never put into the address they open, so those services don't receive it from NetParty. The QR code is made by the extension on your device, not by an online QR service, and is shown in the extension's own window or popup, where the website you're watching can't read it.
  • Local settings. Your name, picture (not an uploaded photo, which stays in this browser only), theme, chat text size, chosen reactions, party and voice settings and server setting are kept in your browser's extension storage (which your browser may sync to your own account if you use browser sync); recently used emoji and party state stay on your device. A party's encryption key is kept only in your browser's memory for that tab, never written to disk or synced, and is deleted when the party ends or you leave it, and at the latest when you close the tab or the browser.

NetParty does not read your streaming account details, passwords, payment information or viewing history. It runs on the supported sites: Netflix, JioHotstar, YouTube, Prime Video (primevideo.com and Amazon's Prime Video pages), HBO Max, Crunchyroll (including its video player frame on static.crunchyroll.com), SonyLIV, Hulu, Disney+, Paramount+, STARZ, Tubi, Twitch, Kick and Rumble. On any other site it runs only on a tab where you click Sync this tab or Join party on this tab, and only on the sites you choose for Always allow on this site (you can turn that off in the popup or your browser's settings). If the video plays inside a player from another site, NetParty asks your browser for access to that player's site first. On those pages it only looks for the video element and reads its play state, position, speed and length.

Update check

A few times a day, NetParty asks netparty.tech which version of the extension is current (/api/version), so it can tell you when an update is out and what's new in it. The request carries nothing about you: no name, no party, no ID and not even your own version number; the comparison happens in your browser. Like any request, it reaches our server with your IP address, which isn't stored.

How long we keep it

While a party is active, the sync server keeps its encrypted data so people who reload can rejoin: the members' encrypted names and pictures and the party's last 150 chat lines (encrypted messages, including replies, reactions, edits and deletes, and notes of who joined, left, was removed or became the host), the encrypted pinned message, and the lock and removed reconnect codes described above. It is deleted when the host ends the party, and any inactive party is removed after at most 6 hours (our hosting provider's point-in-time recovery can keep encrypted copies of the storage for up to 30 days; nobody can read them without the party's key). Basic request logs kept by our hosting provider follow its standard retention; they never contain chat content. The anonymous daily totals are kept for up to 120 days; the daily random value and codes used to count people are deleted when each day ends.

Where it's processed

The sync server runs on Cloudflare Workers. Cloudflare processes connection data, such as IP addresses, to deliver the service under its own privacy policy.
